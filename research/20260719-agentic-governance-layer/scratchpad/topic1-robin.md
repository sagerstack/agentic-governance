# Topic 1 — Runtime controls for the expense-claims agent

**Date:** 2026-07-19  
**Researcher:** Robin (`openai-codex/gpt-5.6-sol`)  
**Status:** Draft (independent P1 artifact)

## Executive summary

- **No control in the requested source set is itself mandatory.** SAFR expressly says it is an industry reference approach, not regulatory guidance or supervisory expectation [S1, pp. 2, 5]. IMDA's 2020 framework is expressly voluntary [S4, pp. 13–14]; its GenAI and Agentic editions present proposed/emerging best practices [S2, pp. 3–4; S3, pp. 3–5]. FEAT is non-prescriptive and Veritas says it does not prescribe compliance steps [S5, p. 4; S6, p. 5]. Applicable laws and requirements remain mandatory, but identifying those laws is outside this topic.
- The strongest runtime architecture is **two complementary layers**: (1) model-input/model-output guardrails at each LLM/VLM call, and (2) a deterministic SAFR-style checkpoint before every effectful action. SAFR explicitly says these layers are complementary, not interchangeable [S1, pp. 14–15].
- For this app, the natural enforcement points are: `preModelHook.py` / `postModelHook.py` for model I/O; `mcpCallTool()` for action envelopes, identity, mandates, dispositions and rate limits; graph gates plus `humanEscalation` for review; and `logEvent` → Seq for governance evidence.
- **Highest priority:** fail-closed authorization of `submitClaim`, `updateClaimStatus`, and `sendNotification`; immutable decision evidence; human-review timeouts and authority; and input/output controls for prompt injection, sensitive-data leakage, unsupported decisions and malformed tool arguments.
- FEAT/Veritas are **not directly in formal scope**: their stated scope is firms using AIDA to provide financial products and services, while this is an internal SUTD expense workflow [S5, p. 5]. Their runtime-adjacent practices remain useful as non-binding analogues for fairness monitoring, explanations and employee recourse.

## Status key

Every row is **Recommended**, not mandated by the cited publication:

- **R-SAFR** — recommended reference-model condition; SAFR is expressly non-regulatory.
- **R-IMDA** — recommended under a voluntary/living IMDA model framework.
- **R-FEAT** — recommended, non-prescriptive FEAT/Veritas practice; not directly in formal scope for this app.

## Runtime control catalogue

### A. Action-time authorization and governance (SAFR + IMDA Agentic AI)

| Control | Source citation | Mandatory vs recommended | Runtime seam | Applicability to this app |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Pre-execution governance checkpoint and authenticated envelope**: declare action type/parameters, actual tool/data/check trace, agent identity, mandate, current state and policy context before execution; validate completeness/coherence and authenticate the envelope against its origin. | SAFR governance envelope [S1, pp. 8–10] | **R-SAFR** | **Tool-use authorization; Audit** | At `mcpCallTool()`, construct the envelope from trusted framework state—not solely agent-authored text—before every MCP invocation. Essential for all three high-impact tools. |
| **Verified, accountable agent identity**: unique cryptographically verifiable identity; bind it to a human/department and acting capacity; centrally catalogue identities and permissions; reject and log unverified identities. | SAFR Agent Identity [S1, p. 10]; IMDA Agentic MGF §2.1.2 [S2, pp. 23–24] | **R-SAFR / R-IMDA** | **Tool-use authorization; Audit** | Give Intake, Compliance, Fraud and Advisor distinct service identities. Do not let a generic app credential obscure which agent initiated claim submission, status change or email. |
| **Explicit machine-readable authority / mandate**: allowed action types, thresholds, conditions, validity window, delegation hierarchy and revocation; authority must not be inferred or enlarged by the model. | SAFR Controls Repository and mandates [S1, pp. 10–11]; IMDA Agentic MGF §2.1.2 [S2, pp. 23–24] | **R-SAFR / R-IMDA** | **Tool-use authorization** | Encode policies such as: Intake may submit only its current user's draft claim; Advisor may set only permitted status transitions; email may go only to policy-approved recipients/templates. |
| **Least privilege enforced structurally at the tool layer**: scoped, time/session-bound, non-transferable authorization; restrict tools, data, read/write operations and calling modes with deterministic controls rather than prompts. | IMDA Agentic MGF §§2.1.2, 2.3.1 [S2, pp. 19, 24, 33–34] | **R-IMDA** | **Tool-use authorization** | Enforce in the `mcpCallTool()` wrapper and MCP servers. Split read vs write DB capabilities and deny tools not assigned to the calling agent. A system-prompt prohibition alone is insufficient. |
| **Deterministic per-action disposition**: evaluate every action against the applicable controls and return one binding result—`Deny`, `Escalate`, `Auto-Execute`, or `Observe`; calibrate using reversibility, materiality, impact, regulatory sensitivity and novelty. Re-authorize every step; a prior approval carries no authority forward. | SAFR Disposition Engine and lifecycle [S1, pp. 11–13] | **R-SAFR** | **Tool-use authorization; Human oversight; Audit** | Put a policy engine in front of each MCP call. A successful policy search must not authorize the later claim submission; each call receives its own disposition and reason. |
| **Exposure, rate and evidence-quality limits**: per-action/aggregate value thresholds, maximum action rate per window, minimum confidence and required evidence; route weak evidence to review irrespective of value. | SAFR control categories [S1, pp. 16–17] | **R-SAFR** | **Tool-use authorization; Human oversight** | Amount/category/currency thresholds guard submission and approval; rate limits stop loops or bulk email; Compliance/Advisor decisions require cited policy evidence and minimum quality before autonomous routing. |
| **Tool/protocol input hardening**: strict typed input formats; trusted MCP-server allowlist; sandbox code execution; filter sensitive data at MCP; use structured schemas for inter-agent messages and limit shared memory. | IMDA Agentic MGF §2.3.1 [S2, p. 34] | **R-IMDA** | **Input; Tool-use authorization; Audit** | Validate MCP arguments against schemas before policy evaluation; allow only the four registered servers; validate Intake → Compliance/Fraud → Advisor state at graph boundaries. No code-execution MCP exists here, so sandboxing is **skip unless one is later added**. |
| **Layered model guardrails plus action authorization**: content moderation, prompt-injection defences and output filtering must run before SAFR-style action authorization; neither layer substitutes for the other. | SAFR stack boundaries [S1, pp. 14–15]; IMDA Agentic MGF §§1.1.1, 2.3.1 [S2, pp. 6–7, 33–34] | **R-SAFR / R-IMDA** | **Input; Output; Tool-use authorization** | Attach I/O guardrails to every OpenRouter LLM/VLM hook, then separately authorize actions at `mcpCallTool()`. A safe-looking sentence can still request an unauthorized claim update. |

### B. Model input/output controls (IMDA GenAI + traditional/Agentic MGF)

| Control | Source citation | Mandatory vs recommended | Runtime seam | Applicability to this app |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Runtime input validation and prompt-attack detection**: domain-specific input filters and guardrails detect unsafe/adversarial instructions; validate harmful responses before they are acted upon. | IMDA GenAI MGF, Trusted Development and Security [S3, pp. 13, 22]; IMDA Agentic MGF §2.3.1 [S2, p. 34] | **R-IMDA** | **Input; Tool-use authorization** | `preModelHook.py` must inspect chat text, receipt OCR/VLM content and retrieved tool content for direct/indirect prompt injection, malformed data and policy bypass instructions. Treat the receipt itself as untrusted multimodal input. |
| **Sensitive-data minimisation and leakage guardrails**: restrict data/tool access, filter sensitive data through MCP, and block inappropriate disclosure in model output. | IMDA Agentic MGF §§2.1.1, 2.3.1 [S2, pp. 15–17, 34–35]; IMDA GenAI MGF, Trusted Development [S3, pp. 13–14] | **R-IMDA** | **Input; Output; Tool-use authorization** | Minimise receipt images, employee IDs, claim history and vendor data sent through OpenRouter; redact or block PII in SSE/chat/email output; prevent cross-claim retrieval. |
| **Evidence-grounded output validation**: use trusted retrieval, input/output filters and confidence/risk signals; require policy evidence and flag uncertainty rather than treating fluent output as factual. | IMDA GenAI MGF, Trusted Development [S3, pp. 13–15]; SAFR evidence-quality controls [S1, p. 17] | **R-IMDA / R-SAFR** | **Output; Human oversight** | `postModelHook.py` should check that Compliance/Advisor findings cite current RAG evidence and that amounts/currency/vendor facts match state. Low confidence or unsupported verdicts go to `humanEscalation`. |
| **Robust exception handling and graceful failure**: detect invalid/unexpected inputs, identify non-repeatable exceptions, keep exception handling aligned with policy, highlight new variables to a human, and actively monitor deployed models. | IMDA 2020 MGF §§3.30–3.35 [S4, pp. 46–48] | **R-IMDA** | **Input; Output; Human oversight** | On OCR ambiguity, unknown currency, missing schema fields, provider timeout or conflicting agent results, do not improvise or silently continue; return a structured failure and escalate/return the claim. |
| **Decision explanations and consequences**: provide understandable reasons for material decisions, the salient data used and consequences; avoid disclosing fraud logic where that would enable gaming. | IMDA 2020 MGF §§3.26–3.29, 3.46–3.48 [S4, pp. 44–45, 53–54]; FEAT principles 13–14 [S5, pp. 6, 12–13] | **R-IMDA; R-FEAT analogue** | **Output; Human oversight** | Advisor output should explain approve/return/escalate and remediation needed. Give less detail for fraud flags where disclosure would enable evasion, but retain the full internal reason in audit. |

### C. Human oversight, failsafes and recourse

| Control | Source citation | Mandatory vs recommended | Runtime seam | Applicability to this app |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Risk-based human checkpoints before consequential actions**: require approval for high-stakes, irreversible, anomalous or user-threshold actions; approval requests should be concise, contextual and expose risk/confidence, with written justification for high risk. | IMDA Agentic MGF §2.2.2 [S2, pp. 29–31]; IMDA 2020 MGF §§3.14–3.18 [S4, pp. 30–31] | **R-IMDA** | **Human oversight; Tool-use authorization** | Gate atypical/high-value claims, sensitive status changes and external email. `humanEscalation` should show the proposed action, evidence, policy/risk result and editable decision—not a raw trace dump. |
| **Substantive escalation contract**: size review capacity; set a response timeout; default to block or senior escalation on timeout; give reviewers explicit approve/modify/decline authority. | SAFR, Activating Human Reviewer Escalation [S1, p. 17] | **R-SAFR** | **Human oversight; Audit** | Persist an escalation task in graph state with deadline and named role. No reply must not become implicit approval; it must block or route upward. |
| **Audit effectiveness of human oversight**: track override/modify rates and review response times, detect outlier reviewers, train reviewers on failure modes, and ensure domain expertise. | IMDA Agentic MGF §2.2.2 [S2, pp. 29–30] | **R-IMDA** | **Human oversight; Audit** | Emit reviewer decisions and latency to Seq. Very low override rates or implausibly fast reviews should trigger oversight-quality investigation, not be treated as success. |
| **Fail closed and contain malfunction**: when approval infrastructure is unavailable or an action has no established policy, deny by default; for severe anomalies halt/terminate agent execution and use fallback procedures. | IMDA Agentic MGF §§2.2.2, 2.3.3 [S2, pp. 30, 43–44] | **R-IMDA** | **Tool-use authorization; Human oversight; Audit** | Add deny-by-default for unknown tools/actions; circuit-break repeated calls; halt the affected graph run and route to manual processing. | **Employee recourse and correction**: provide a channel to query, appeal and request human review; accept verified relevant supplementary information; retain decision context. | FEAT principles 10–11 [S5, pp. 6, 10–11]; IMDA 2020 MGF §§3.52–3.54 [S4, pp. 56–57] | **R-FEAT analogue / R-IMDA** | **Output; Human oversight; Audit** | In the claim UI, let the employee correct extracted receipt facts and appeal a return/denial. Feed verified corrections into a human review rather than letting the same model automatically reaffirm itself. |

| Control | Source citation | Mandatory vs recommended | Runtime seam | Applicability to this app |
|---|---|---|---|---|

## Recommended target implementation order

1. Wrap `mcpCallTool()` with a SAFR-style gateway: trusted envelope → identity → controls/mandate → deterministic disposition → append-only audit.
2. First policies: deny unknown action/tool; least privilege by agent; strict schemas; claim ownership/status transition rules; amount/currency/evidence/rate thresholds; mandatory review for anomalous/high-impact cases.
3. Put prompt-injection/PII/input checks in `preModelHook.py` and grounding/PII/schema/decision-evidence checks in `postModelHook.py`; keep this separate from tool authorization.
4. Turn `humanEscalation` into a persisted escalation contract with evidence, named authority, timeout, fail-closed default and reviewer-effectiveness metrics.
5. Upgrade Seq events into a tamper-evident correlated governance record and wire alert types to concrete `observe`, `escalate`, `deny`, halt and fallback actions.
6. Add employee-facing explanation/correction/appeal flow and continuous accuracy, drift and fairness monitoring as non-binding FEAT/Veritas analogues.
